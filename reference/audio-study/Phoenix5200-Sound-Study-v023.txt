PHOENIX 5200 — MUSIC CHECK AND EXPLOSION FADE, v0.23
2026-11-06 UTC

Music clarification
The arcade shield DOES stop the opening music. The original shield sound
routine at $3B28 writes a low-valued SoundControlB byte, clearing its tune
selection bits. The recorded MAME shield-input trace confirms it: frame 720
has tune 3; frame 721 has tune 0, shield count 63 and hit count 0. Entering
wave one itself does not stop the music. Bird-body hit sounds also clear the
tune bits, and the original phase timeout at $3A82 remains in place.

The tentative change that forced uninterrupted opening music was incorrect
and was reverted before publication. v0.23 retains v0.22's arcade music
cancellation behavior. Tests explicitly check shooting (music continues),
bird hits, shield activation and the phase timeout (music stops).

Explosion comparison and change
The ship/mothership death-noise envelope was rounded to six bits at every
update. Its low-level decay became a sequence of forced one-unit drops,
ending the audible tail too early. v0.23 keeps eight-bit capacitor precision
and converts to POKEY volume only at output. The attack/release constants
remain 0.009044 and 0.3196 seconds, derived from the reference circuit.
The low noise frequency and maximum volume are unchanged.

The same 64-tick explosion command was compared in the checked-in MAME-derived
circuit model and in actual Altirra POKEY recordings of v0.22 and v0.23.
At 1.8 seconds after onset, normalized RMS over 100 ms was about:
  Arcade model: 0.079
  v0.22:        0.000
  v0.23:        0.111
This shows the restored late tail; it is not a claim of identical timbre.
The four-bit volume steps and POKEY noise remain approximations of the arcade.
No EQ or normalization was applied to the distributed WAV files; normalization
was used only for the stated comparison metric. Bird-hit tones were inspected
but are unchanged by this narrow revision.

Validation
A 39.52-second controller-driven Altirra run included firing, hits, shield,
ship explosion, respawn and entry into wave two. It had zero missed sound
updates, zero active-play queue underruns, no late VBI and no graphics
allocation faults. The native recording duration matched emulated time.
The existing 4,200 music-tick, 1,744 pitch, 8,192 large-bird cadence and 2,048
quiet-formation checks also passed, plus the explicit music-cancellation tests.
This is not a new full-game completion or physical-console test.
